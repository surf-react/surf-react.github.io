Everything we study shares one setting: the few ångströms where a solid meets a gas or a liquid. Within that setting, our work falls into four connected lines.

## Heterogeneous catalysis from first principles

We map out elementary steps of catalytic reactions on metal and oxide surfaces with density functional theory — adsorption, diffusion, bond breaking and making — and assemble them into microkinetic models that can be compared directly with experimental rates and selectivities.

## Machine-learned interatomic potentials

DFT is accurate but expensive. We train neural-network and kernel-based potentials on ab initio data to reach the length and time scales of realistic interfaces: high adsorbate coverages, defects, steps, and explicit solvents.

## Electrochemical interfaces

Electrified metal–electrolyte interfaces power fuel cells, electrolysers, and batteries. We model the double layer, potential-dependent adsorption, and proton-coupled electron transfer steps to understand and predict electrocatalytic activity.

## Reactions in confined and 2D environments

Single-atom catalysts, 2D materials, and porous frameworks confine reactants in unusual geometries. We explore how confinement and support effects reshape reaction energetics compared to extended surfaces.

---

**Methods we use:** periodic DFT (VASP, CP2K), ab initio and machine-learning molecular dynamics, nudged elastic band and metadynamics for reaction paths, microkinetic modelling, and good old statistical thermodynamics.
