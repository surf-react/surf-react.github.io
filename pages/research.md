Everything we study shares one setting: the few ångströms where a molecule touches a solid surface. Within that setting, our work falls into four connected parts.

## Heterogeneous catalysis from first principles

We map out elementary steps of catalytic reactions on metal and oxide surfaces with density functional theory — adsorption, diffusion, bond breaking and making — and assemble them into microkinetic models that can be compared directly with experimental rates and selectivities.

## Reactions in confined and 2D environments

Single-atom catalysts, 2D materials, and porous frameworks confine reactants in unusual geometries. We explore how confinement and support effects reshape reaction energetics compared to extended surfaces.

## Method development

Parametrizing new surface-suitable forcefields for accurate QM/MM calculations, training of machine-learned interatomic potentials for QM/ML, and improving classical 2D relaxed scans.

## Tight collaboration with scanning probe microscopy

SPM under ultra-high vacuum yields the ultimate control over reaction conditions and resolution of imaging down to single bonds. A direct comparison of simulated nc-AFM and STM images with experiments allows for model justification and a detailed insight into electronic structure.

---

**Methods we use:** periodic DFT (VASP, FHI-AIMS), ab initio and machine-learning molecular dynamics, nudged elastic band, umbrella-sampling, and metadynamics for reaction paths, and good old statistical thermodynamics.
